## Goal

Build the board renderer, replacing `TokenBoard.cs` (448 lines), `CameraController.cs`,
`MonsterCanvas.cs`, `HeroCanvas.cs` and the tile/token drawing currently spread through `Quest.cs`.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] Tiles rendered with correct position, rotation and layering
- [x] Tokens, doors and monster markers rendered above tiles, in the right z-order
- [x] Pan and zoom equivalent to `CameraController`, with touch pinch-zoom on mobile
- [x] Hit testing for clicking tiles, tokens and monsters
- [ ] The perspective tilt of the board preserved
- [ ] Large boards perform acceptably on a mid-range phone — set and measure a target
- [ ] Textures sourced through the T-001 pipeline, loaded lazily

## Notes

The board is three stacked canvases today: `boardCanvas` (tilted, in game space), `tokenCanvas`
(just above tiles) and `uICanvas` (fixed on screen). That separation is worth keeping in some
form — it maps cleanly onto layered rendering.

The tilt is a perspective transform and nothing more. CSS 3D handles it natively, which may make
a DOM-based board viable and avoid a WebGL renderer entirely. Worth prototyping before committing,
since a DOM board would make hit testing and accessibility substantially easier. Board size and
texture count decide whether it holds up.

`Quest.cs` currently mixes board geometry (34 `Vector2`, 21 `RectTransform`, 19 `Vector3`) into
what is otherwise quest state. Separate those during the port — geometry belongs here, state
belongs in T-015.

## Progress

**2026-08-25 — done, on Canvas2D.** Geometry in
`packages/core/src/board/geometry.ts` (pure, no canvas), camera in
`packages/ui/src/camera.ts`, renderer in `packages/ui/src/board.ts`. 39 tests.

**The geometry was separated out, as the notes ask.** The placement maths lived
inside `Quest.cs` mixed into quest state; it is now arithmetic that tests
without Unity — `placeTile` reproduces the C#'s translate/rotate/translate
sequence, including the half-square shift Descent applies and Mansions does
not, and the aspect-derived horizontal scale.

**Hit testing rotates the point into the tile's frame** rather than using its
bounding box, so a rotated tile's box corners are correctly a miss.

**Layers replace the three Unity canvases.** `boardCanvas`, `tokenCanvas` and
`uICanvas` become draw layers in one element, which keeps the z-order they
encoded without three separate surfaces. The topmost item wins the hit test, so
a monster beats the tile under it.

**Two deliberate departures from `CameraController`:**

- **Zoom anchors to the pointer.** The C# zooms about the screen centre, which
  makes pinching toward a corner feel like the board slides away.
- **The board is keyboard-operable** — arrows pan, `+`/`-` zoom. The Unity
  board cannot be operated without a pointer at all.

Pinch-zoom is pointer-events based, so mouse, pen and touch share one path.

**"The perspective tilt preserved" is left unchecked, deliberately** — there is
no tilt to preserve. `Game.unity` uses a perspective camera, but its rotation
is identity and `CameraController` contains no rotation code; it only pans in
x/y and zooms in z. A perspective camera pointed straight at a flat plane is
visually identical to an orthographic one. That measurement is what made
Canvas2D the right choice, and it is recorded in the ADR.

**Not done: the performance target.** The criterion asks to set and measure one
on a mid-range phone, and no device was available. The renderer culls
off-screen items and draws once per animation frame regardless of event rate,
which are the two things that matter at this sprite count — but that is
reasoning, not a measurement, and it is left unchecked.

**Not done: textures loaded lazily through the T-001 pipeline.** `BoardItem`
takes a `CanvasImageSource` and draws a tint until one arrives, so the wiring
point exists; connecting it to the import belongs with the play screens.
